Is my 2026 assessment fair?

Most Mineral Point assessments jumped this year because the City re-valued every home to today's prices. A big increase isn't automatically unfair. What matters is whether your home is assessed at the same share of its value as everyone else's. This page helps you check in about five minutes.

Made by a Mineral Point resident to help neighbors check their own assessments. It is not from the City of Mineral Point or Equity Appraisal, and it is not an appraisal or legal advice.

It can't see condition: a dated home and a renovated one look alike to this math. Treat a high ratio as a reason to look closer, not proof.

The one idea behind it

Wisconsin law says every home must be assessed at the same percentage of what it would sell for. So the test is a simple division:

Your assessed value÷Your home's market value=Your ratio

Source: Wisconsin Department of Revenue, 2026 Guide for Property Owners (PB-060), Section III, "Equitable Assessment," page 10.

Recent Mineral Point sales are assessed at about 99% of their value, on average. If your ratio is close to that, your assessment is in line with your neighbors'. If it's well above, you may be paying more than your share.

    If your ratio is high: what to do next

    Most people never get past step 1. Calling is free, and errors on the property record (wrong square footage, bathrooms, basement) are often fixed on the spot.

    1. Start here

      Call the assessor

      Equity Appraisal, LLC does the City's assessments. Useful things to ask for:

      • Your property record card. It lists what the City has on file: square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, basement, garage, condition. Check every line for errors.
      • The sales they used to value your home, with the date and price of each.
      • How your value was calculated: the land value, the building value, and any adjustment applied to your neighborhood.

      These are public records. If you can't get them from the assessor, the City Clerk can provide them.

      equityappraisalsm@yahoo.com · 608-826-0009

    2. At least 48 hours before the Board meets

      Tell the City Clerk you plan to object

      If talking doesn't resolve it, you can appeal to the Board of Review. For 2026 it meets Wednesday, October 14. You must give the clerk notice first.

      cityclerk@cityofmineralpointwi.gov · 608-987-2361

    3. The clerk asked for it by October 12

      Fill out the objection form

      Form PA-115A asks what you think your home is worth and why. Send the completed form to the City Clerk.

      Form PA-115A (PDF)

    4. At the hearing

      Bring your evidence

      Bring copies of whatever supports your value, such as sales of similar homes or your purchase records. The state's guide explains how the hearing works.

      State guide for property owners (PDF)
